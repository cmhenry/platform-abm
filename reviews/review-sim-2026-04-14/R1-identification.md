# Reviewer 1: Identification
*Simulated review — generated 2026-04-14*
*Persona: methods and identification focus*

---

**Summary**

This paper adapts the Tiebout citizen-sorting model to explain cross-platform cycling by extremist communities. Platforms are reconceptualized as quasi-governments offering public-goods bundles, and communities as mobile agents sorting across three governance regimes (direct voting, coalition formation, algorithmic recommendation). Using an ABM in a 3×3×3 factorial design, the paper argues that the concentration-raid-retreat cycle is an emergent equilibrium of the sorting process, that platform diversification protects mainstream communities at a premium that grows with extremist threat, and that coalition governance provides structural immunity through endogenous enclave formation.

---

**Strengths**

The model is formally specified and internally consistent. Key parameters are defined with clear utility-function representations, equilibrium conditions are stated, and the three governance types map onto recognizable real-world structures with corresponding citations. The paper avoids a common failure mode in computational social science: it does not conflate the ABM with an empirical claim about any specific platform and explicitly flags the model as a theoretical device for mechanism identification. The factorial design is well-chosen: crossing platform count, extremist proportion, and parasitism intensity generates testable interaction predictions rather than a collection of unrelated runs. The displacement paradox (displaced communities land on better-matched platforms) is a genuinely counterintuitive result that would not emerge from a non-dynamic model, and it is correctly reported alongside the conditions under which it reverses.

---

**Weaknesses**

**Major — fundamental limitation that should be acknowledged more directly**

*The search protocol severs the link between the theoretical construct and the causal mechanism the paper claims to demonstrate.*

The central empirical claim is that the raiding cycle is an "emergent property of decentralized sorting, not coordinated strategy." This claim depends on communities having genuinely limited information — they move on blind base-utility comparisons without observing platform composition. But the model bundles together two distinct mechanisms that the design cannot separate: (1) structural sorting incentives generated by governance-bundle differences, and (2) tipping dynamics generated by simultaneous relocation of all communities at each step. The mass-departure bursts described in Result 7 (94–100% of outflow in burst events) are a direct consequence of the simultaneity assumption in Step 5 of the Tiebout cycle, not only of the payoff structure. When all relocations fire at once, individual utility calculations that each marginally favor leaving aggregate into coordinated-looking mass departures. A sequential or staggered relocation rule would produce different burst statistics. The paper acknowledges simultaneity as a modeling choice but does not test whether the raiding-cycle result survives relaxing it. Because the raiding-cycle claim is in the abstract and conclusion, and because a substantial policy argument (disrupting coordination channels is insufficient; structural intervention is required) rests on demonstrating emergence rather than coordination, the failure to test this sensitivity is a major gap. *Addressable by adding a staggered-relocation variant to the existing sensitivity analysis.*

**Major — addressable within the current design**

*The sensitivity analysis is planned but not reported.*

Section 3.3 describes a systematic OAT sensitivity analysis and second-order Sobol indices. The limitations section acknowledges these are "not yet reported." Results 1–7 rest on a specific parameterization that is theoretically motivated but not calibrated to observable data. Given that the paper's policy claims scale with the magnitude of the diversification premium and the enclave-formation homogeneity statistics, the absence of reported sensitivity is not a minor gap. The direction of the enclave-formation finding (Result 5) could plausibly reverse under alternative coalition parameters (g, m, μ), since the number and mutation rate of candidate coalitions directly determines how easily extremist-dominated coalitions can outcompete mainstream ones. Until these results are available, the robustness claims in the abstract and summary remain asserted rather than demonstrated.

**Major — addressable within the current design**

*The operationalization of ρ_e conflates ideologue and griefer counts.*

The paper explicitly distinguishes ideologue communities (low α) from griefer communities (high α) in the model specification, but in the experimental design, α is varied as a single scalar applied uniformly to all extremist communities. The composite ρ_e parameter then conflates the proportion of ideologues with the proportion of griefers. If 10% of communities are extremists at α = 2, they are all ideologues; at α = 10, all are griefers. A realistic scenario involves a mix. Disaggregated runs varying the ideologue-griefer composition at fixed ρ_e and α would strengthen the behavioral-regime claim in Result 4. *Addressable with additional experimental configurations.*

**Minor — addressable**

*Normalized utility across types is not fully justified.* Mainstream utility is bounded in [−α, N_a] and extremist utility in [0, N_a + α]. An extremist community achieving normalized utility of 0.6 is capturing 60% of N_a + α, a portion of which is welfare transferred from mainstream communities. A mainstream community achieving 0.6 has lost nothing to parasitism in that scenario. Presenting these as comparable quantities without noting the welfare-transfer content is misleading. At minimum, a decomposition of extremist normalized utility into base-match and parasitism components would clarify what the efficiency figures are actually measuring.

**Minor — addressable**

*ANOVA reporting discipline is not applied consistently.* The paper reports F-statistics, p-values, and η² for the three-way ANOVA and correctly directs attention to η² rather than p-values given the large N. The same discipline is not applied to bootstrap confidence interval reporting elsewhere, where raw utility deltas are presented without power analysis or simulation-sample-size justification. The interaction terms (6.4% of variance) are identified at F = 92 with 5,400 observations — sensitivity to small true effects is high, and the practical significance of this interaction deserves more direct discussion.

---

**Questions for the Authors**

1. Does the raiding-cycle finding (Result 7, 94–100% burst concentration) survive when relocation is staggered rather than simultaneous? The burst statistic may be an artifact of the simultaneity assumption rather than evidence of structural emergence. Have you tested any alternative movement rules?

2. The coalition enclave settling time distributions show that at high α, settling extends past step 90. Since t_max = 100, some iterations may not have achieved enclave equilibrium by simulation end. How many iterations at α = 10 reach t_max without settling, and how does excluding those affect the enclave homogeneity statistics?

3. What governs which 25% of direct platforms absorb disproportionate raiding pressure (Result 7, slope distribution)? Is this explained by initial community composition, bundle initialization, or stochastic arrival order? If the failure mode is concentrated in a predictable subset of platforms, this has direct design implications worth developing.

4. The paper claims that communities evaluate destination platforms using only base utility (Step 3 of the Tiebout cycle), without observing platform composition. But governance update (Step 1) occurs before search (Step 3), meaning the bundle B_q that communities observe already reflects the current tenant composition of platform q. Can communities infer composition from bundle shifts, even under the stated information restriction?

5. Given that the sensitivity analysis is planned but not reported, which single parameter do you expect would most threaten the direction of the coalition-enclave finding if varied substantially from its baseline value?

---

**Recommendation: Revise and Resubmit**

The theoretical contribution is genuine and the simulation design is more carefully constructed than most ABM work in this space. But two major gaps — the unreported sensitivity analysis and the untested sensitivity of the raiding-cycle claim to the simultaneity assumption — leave the paper's core empirical claims inadequately supported. Both are addressable within the current design without fundamental reconceptualization.
